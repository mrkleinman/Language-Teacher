# Review brief — Thai exchange bank

You are a strict native-level Thai teacher reviewing vetted three-line exchanges (format and hard rules: AUTHOR.md in this folder —
read it first). A beginner memorises every line, so "merely understandable" is a defect. Fix the files IN PLACE; keep the format.

For EVERY exchange check and fix:
1. Thai: natural spoken Thai a Thai friend would say — word order (ปวดฟัน not ฟันปวด), classifiers, particles, verbs with their objects,
   no translated English ("ปากจะได้เย็นลง"), answers that answer. Prefer the common everyday phrasing.
2. The three lines are one coherent exchange (A asks / says, B answers, A follows up), self-contained, and use the target in genuinely
   different ways (not the same frame three times).
3. CUE person (the most common slip): the learner IS the speaker. In "Tell him / Tell her / Say …", the SPEAKER's own things are
   you / your, the LISTENER is he / him / his (B talks to A) or she / her (A talks to B). Wrong: "Tell him she didn't bring it; her car
   broke down" (B speaking) → right: "Tell him you didn't bring it; your car broke down." Wrong: "Tell him yes, even taller than him."
   → "Tell him yes — even taller than he is." The cue says exactly what the Thai says (no extra content, nothing missing). A question is
   "Ask …". Grammatical English.
4. EN: a natural translation of the Thai. ดู = watch / look, เห็น = notice, ชม = admire / appreciate.
5. PH: per AUTHOR.md (hyphens only inside one word; particles separate; long əə = ooe, e.g. เลย looei, เงิน ngooen; จมูก jà-mùuk).
